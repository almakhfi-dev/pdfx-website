Why Convert Images to PDF with PDFx?

While image formats like JPG and PNG are great for photos and web graphics, they present notable challenges when sharing multi-page documents, official applications, or academic coursework. A multi-page PDF solves these issues by bundling everything into a single, standardized document with consistent layout and formatting across all operating systems.

In-Browser Image Processing

The biggest difference between PDFx and conventional online converters is our architecture. Traditional online tools upload your private pictures to an unknown cloud server, convert them remotely, and send back a link. This introduces severe security vulnerabilities for sensitive documents such as passport scans, bank slips, medical records, or company invoices.

PDFx performs all image embedding, canvas rendering, and PDF compilation locally inside your web browser using HTML5 Canvas and JavaScript. Selected images are processed locally; the website also makes standard requests for hosting, libraries, and advertising. Local processing alone does not establish regulatory compliance.

Image Resizing & Compression

PDFx fits images onto A4 pages. Images are resized to a maximum edge of 1700 pixels and encoded as JPEG with adaptive compression, starting at a quality setting of 0.72 and potentially reducing it to control file size. PNG transparency is placed on a white background. Resizing and compression can reduce detail or alter colors; review the downloaded PDF before sharing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I combine different image formats into one PDF?

Yes. You can select a mix of JPG, JPEG, and PNG images simultaneously. PDFx will compile all of them seamlessly into a single sequential PDF document.

Is there any limit on how many images I can convert?

There is no fixed limit. You can convert dozens of images at once. The practical capacity depends entirely on your device's available memory.

Will my images lose quality during conversion?

Images may be resized and JPEG-compressed to keep output manageable. This can reduce fine detail or affect colors. The output contains image pages, without OCR or added searchable text.

Are my images stored or analyzed?

Never. All processing takes place locally in your browser memory. We do not have servers storing your files or scanning your content.

Can I change the order of pages before downloading?
